`healthcare-org` is organised the way a hospital group's software estate is organised: by business domain, not by technical layer. Each domain owns its own Postgres database, its own services, and its own vocabulary of events.

Services are numbered by domain. The port block tells you where a service sits before you know anything else about it — `85xx` is pharmacy, `87xx` is revenue cycle.

|---|---|---|---|
| `80xx` | **Platform** | 10 | Identity, auth, authorization, config, secrets, feature flags, tenancy, audit, service registry |
| `81xx` | **Patients** | 10 | The system of record for demographics, consent, preferences, relationships, timeline, search, and merge |
| `82xx` | **Providers** | 9 | Clinician records, credentialing, licensing, specialties, schedules, on-call rotas, performance |
| `83xx` | **Clinical / EHR** | 15 | Encounters, notes, problem lists, medication reconciliation, allergies, immunizations, vitals, CPOE, care plans, referrals, discharge, decision support, diagnosis codes, care teams |
| `84xx` | **Diagnostics** | 8 | Lab and imaging orders and results, pathology, radiology worklist, specimen tracking, reference ranges |
| `85xx` | **Pharmacy** | 7 | Prescriptions, refills, drug interactions, formulary, inventory, dispensing |
| `86xx` | **Scheduling** | 5 | Appointments, slots, reminders, waitlist, room booking |
| `87xx` | **Billing / RCM** | 10 | Charge capture, coding, claims submission and adjudication, denials, invoicing, payments, statements, collections |
| `88xx` | **Insurance** | 6 | Eligibility, prior authorization, coverage verification, payer directory, EDI connect, claims status |
| `89xx` | **Devices / IoT** | 5 | Device registry and fleet, telemetry ingest, threshold alerting, remote monitoring |

The authoritative catalog is [`registry.yaml`](https://github.com/healthcare-org-app/healthcare-infra/blob/main/registry.yaml) in `healthcare-infra`: one row per service with its port, language, HTTP dependencies, and Kafka topics.

## Who everything depends on

Domains are not equal. A handful of services carry most of the fleet, which is what makes a change to any of them interesting:

| Inbound HTTP callers | Service | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 94 | `audit-log-service` | Every service writes to the audit trail |
| 36 | `patients-service` | Nearly every clinical and financial record hangs off a patient |
| 21 | `providers-service` | Attribution and authorization |
| 12 | `notifications-service`, `ehr-service` | Outbound comms; clinical record lookups |
| 8 | `auth-service` | Token introspection |

`patients-service` is also the hand-written **golden reference** — the other 100 were generated from a template against it, so it is the one to read first.

## Data ownership

Fifteen Postgres databases, one per domain: `identity`, `auth`, `patients`, `providers`, `ehr`, `lab`, `imaging`, `pharmacy`, `insurance`, `claims`, `billing`, `notifications`, `devices`, `ai_agents`, `audit`.

No service reads another domain's database. Cross-domain reads are HTTP calls; cross-domain writes are events. Where a service needs fast local access to a peer's data — `patient-search-service` needing patient names, say — it subscribes to that peer's events and keeps its own snapshot, which it then has to keep correct when `patient.merged` fires.

## Events

Twenty-five Kafka topics, declared in [`topics.yaml`](https://github.com/healthcare-org-app/healthcare-infra/blob/main/topics.yaml):

**Identity** `identity.user.created` · `identity.user.deactivated` · `auth.session.started`
**Patients** `patient.created` · `patient.updated` · `patient.merged` · `patient.consent.updated`
**Clinical** `encounter.started` · `encounter.ended` · `order.placed`
**Diagnostics** `lab.result.available` · `imaging.result.available`
**Pharmacy** `prescription.issued` · `prescription.refill_requested`
**Scheduling** `appointment.booked` · `appointment.cancelled`
**Billing** `charge.captured` · `claim.submitted` · `claim.adjudicated` · `invoice.issued` · `invoice.paid`
**Devices** `device.reading` · `device.alert.triggered`
**Comms** `notification.requested`
**Audit** `audit.event`

`device.reading` and `audit.event` each run 24 partitions — they carry an order of magnitude more traffic than the business topics.

## How a service is shaped

The fleet is uniform by construction, generated from one template in `healthcare-tools`. Every Python service:

- exposes a Flask HTTP API with JWT-guarded resource routes, plus `/health` and `/ready` injected by the shared runtime
- owns one Postgres database, storing records as a fixed envelope (`id`, `status`, `created_at`, `updated_at`) around a free-form JSONB `data` document
- publishes domain events and subscribes to peers' over Kafka
- calls siblings through a shared retry + circuit-breaker HTTP client
- writes every mutation to the append-only `audit.event` stream

The shared runtime is [`healthcare-common`](https://github.com/healthcare-org-app/healthcare-common): HTTP client, event bus, JWT auth, correlation-id tracing, DB pool, audit publisher, and the `create_service()` bootstrap that wires them together. A service's main file is about fifteen lines.

<Note>
The uniformity is the point, and also the catch. Because 100 services were generated from one template, they are near-identical apart from three constants — which makes fleet-wide tooling easy to demonstrate, and makes any single service a poor showcase of API design variety. The bespoke endpoints are where the interesting differences live.
</Note>
